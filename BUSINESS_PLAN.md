# Business plan: independent service-business decision tools

The initial business serves freelancers and small service firms making pricing, scope and near-term cash decisions. Three downloadable tools share a browser-based, offline-friendly delivery format. Customers buy practical decision support, clear instructions and reusable exports. This is a **commercial hypothesis**, not a verified market opportunity. No external sale, ad, account creation or store change has occurred.

## Offer and sequencing

Lead with Service Price Lab at an experimental $39. Show how utilization changes the sustainable hourly price, using transparent fictional examples. Follow with Project Scope Studio ($39) for proposal work and Cash Runway Planner ($49) for weekly cash visibility. Offer the three-product bundle at an experimental $89 only after checking bundle contribution and fulfillment. Prices are recommendations; research access has not established competitive pricing.

Use existing Shopify as preferred storefront, subject to owner approval and an actual plan/app check. Offline downloads avoid an initial SaaS hosting commitment; they do not prevent copying and should carry a practical per-business license. Do not promise financial results, personalized advice, tax compliance or professional certifications. Avoid confidential or employer-derived material and every excluded healthcare category.

## Economic logic and constraints

Digital reproduction has near-zero marginal file cost, but acquisition, refunds, support, payment fees and maintenance remain material. `financial-models/RESULTS.md` models required orders and traffic; its assumptions do not predict sales. Initial validation ceiling is $1,000 and no spending is currently authorized. A downloadable tool is scalable only if customers understand inputs, trust outputs and buy at a contribution-positive acquisition cost.

Free alternatives are a serious threat. Differentiation hypothesis: focused connected workflows, plain-language assumptions, scenario comparisons, validated calculations, exports and no account requirement. Test these differences with buyers rather than claiming competitors lack them.

## Distribution and operational model

Prepare intent-focused pages around pricing overhead, billable utilization, freelancer scope contingency and weekly cash timing. Each page should teach one practical method with an original example and clear limitations. Search traffic will take work and is not forecast. Etsy could provide discovery but needs current category/policy/fee checks; Gumroad/Payhip are alternative delivery channels rather than guaranteed acquisition engines.

Track per-product orders, refunds, ad cost, attributed sessions, support time and contribution using CSV until approved integrations exist. Separate synthetic demo data from actual records. Review pricing and funnel weekly during a pilot; monthly afterward if stable. Routine packaging, QA, catalog exports and analytics preparation can be automated locally. Publication, refunds, billing decisions, factual claims, customer privacy and spending remain controlled actions.

## Decision gates

Proceed to controlled paid validation only after owner approval, manual compatibility/accessibility checks and store test-order delivery. A positive interview response does not pass commercial validation. At least five independent paid customers at the intended price plus observed contribution, fulfillment and manageable support are a small pilot signal, not proof of a large market. Retire or revise an offer if prospective buyers consistently prefer existing free workflows, use differs from assumptions, or measured CAC exceeds contribution before acquisition.

Long-term $200,000–$300,000 annual owner earnings remain a target. Capacity, tax, legal compliance, actual recurring demand, attribution and distribution must be demonstrated before extrapolation.
