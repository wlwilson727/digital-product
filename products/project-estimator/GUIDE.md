# Project Scope Studio — customer guide

## Who this helps
Freelance designers, developers, writers and consultants quoting fixed-scope work. Use it to connect deliverables, effort, risk allowance and payment terms before sending a proposal. It is not a contract, invoice system or time tracker.

## Workflow
1. Open index.html and review the fictional example.
2. Enter your labor cost per hour. This is the amount you want to recover for labor before additional margin, not necessarily your public hourly rate. If you enter your public rate, margin adds another uplift; understand that before quoting.
3. Break work into concrete deliverables with estimated hours and direct project costs. Include discovery, communication, review, QA and handover.
4. Write included outcomes, excluded work and revision boundaries. Be specific about quantity, format and acceptance conditions.
5. Choose contingency for uncertainty, margin for surplus and a deposit share. Review the effective margin after any discount.
6. Export the estimate and transfer the final agreed scope and payment terms into your independently reviewed contract.
7. When the client changes scope, estimate extra hours and costs. Obtain written approval before performing additional work.

## Formulas
Task base cost = hours × labor cost rate + direct costs. Total base cost is the sum of tasks. Contingency allowance = base cost × contingency percentage. Pre-discount quote = (base cost + contingency) ÷ (1 − target margin). Quote = pre-discount quote × (1 − discount). Deposit = quote × deposit share; balance = quote − deposit. Effective margin = (quote − base cost − contingency) ÷ quote, or undefined when quote is zero. Additional change quote = (change hours × labor rate + change direct costs) × (1 + contingency) ÷ (1 − margin). It does not inherit the original discount. Combined quote adds the original quote and change quote; it does not adjust deposit or payments already collected.

## Fictional example
Cedar & Finch is an invented client requesting a five-page brochure site. The example includes 33 hours at $65 cost per hour, $60 in direct costs, 15% contingency, 20% margin and 40% deposit. A separate four-hour change illustrates a scope extension. The figures are not market rates or a recommendation for every freelancer.

## Scope workshop
**Deliverable test:** For each task, write what the client receives and how you know it is done. Replace vague activities like “marketing support” with bounded outputs.
**Assumption test:** List dependencies you do not control: client content, approvals, access and third-party review. Explain how delays affect the schedule in the contract.
**Revision test:** Define a feedback round, who consolidates it and what counts as a new direction. State a change-approval process and obtain written agreement.
**Discount test:** Reduce the quote by 10% and inspect effective margin. Discounts reduce surplus faster than they reduce price. Agree to a discount only with a clear commercial reason or reduced scope.
**Retrospective:** After completion, compare actual hours with estimates and record which assumptions failed. Update future estimates based on your own data.

## Limitations
No sales tax/VAT, payment fees, schedule/resource planning, automatic proposal PDF, signatures, legal clauses or account receivables are included. Enter direct costs once; overhead can be reflected in your labor cost but is not modeled separately. Contingency applies to both labor and direct costs. Profit margin is a share of price, not a markup percentage. Zero-hour tasks and zero quotes are permitted and should be interpreted deliberately. Estimates are only as good as assumptions. Scope notes are documentation aids, not legal advice. General numeric inputs are limited to one trillion and estimates to 100 task rows; percentages have the narrower limits shown in the form.

## Saving and exports
Inputs are saved locally when browser storage is available. JSON preserves editable task rows and scope notes. CSV contains the itemized costs, scope and quote outputs; it is a report, not an import format. Print uses the entered assumptions and current results. Use JSON backups and protect exports containing client information.
