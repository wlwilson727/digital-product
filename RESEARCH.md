Source links are research leads, not citations supporting factual demand claims.

## Scope and scoring

Eighteen niches are compared in `research/niches.csv`, including all fifteen requested categories and three additional niches. Each record identifies customers, problem hypotheses, reference alternatives, proposed price, gaps, effort, support, acquisition and legal risks. Advertised prices and purchase evidence remain explicitly unknown. Scores use 1–5 ratings with demand 25%, profit potential 20%, differentiation 15%, automation 15%, acquisition feasibility 15%, simplicity 10%. **Every demand score is 1 because evidence is unavailable.** Other ratings are analyst judgments about unit economics and workflow complexity, not measured market facts. The score is prioritization for validation, not a profitability estimate. Market saturation and customer complaints cannot responsibly be quantified with this access limit; presumed free substitutes and common generic alternatives are risks to investigate.

## Top five and build decision

1. Service-business pricing: a concrete decision tied to revenue, transparent arithmetic, simple distribution, possible adjacent products. A buyer may still prefer a free spreadsheet; willingness to pay is unknown.
2. 13-week cashflow: foreseeable value in payment-timing decisions and recurring weekly use. Competes against free cashflow templates; acquisition and interpretation support are risks.
3. Freelancer project estimates: scope and contingency connect directly to proposal work, with an export buyers can use. Free estimate templates reduce pricing power.
4. Industry-specific profitability: potentially differentiated but niche language and interviews are needed before building custom assumptions.
5. Craft-market booth planning: targeted buyer identity and quantifiable event decisions, but seasonality and low willingness to pay are unverified.

Selected products: **Service Price Lab ($39), Cash Runway Planner ($49), Project Scope Studio ($39)**, plus a proposed $89 bundle. The first three share a service-business buyer and create a connected pricing → project estimate → cash timing workflow. Shared code and simple downloadable browser delivery reduce marginal production costs; quantitative customer decisions offer a clearer validation signal than generic planners. All recommended prices are experiments, not observed competitor prices. Selection reflects reasonable problem and economic hypotheses in the absence of usable demand evidence; it must be revisited after interviews and purchase tests.

Development hours are independent rough estimates, not logged actual labor. Production tooling can run locally without paid APIs. Maintenance includes browser regressions, calculation checks, documentation and source review. Prospective competitors have not been inspected; do not describe their quality as poor or their products as inferior.

## Validation needed before market claims

For each selected niche, record at least five accessible direct competitor listings, dated advertised price, included formats, last update, refunds/support conditions and exact public review wording. Distinguish reviews from sales counts and sales counts from revenue. Seek five consented interviews per target segment; ask for the last pricing/cashflow/scope decision and present workflow rather than leading questions. Test actual purchase intent with approved storefront experiments; page visits and email signups are weak proxies. Use the staged plan in `marketing/VALIDATION_PLAN.md`.

Do not include healthcare IT, hospital technology, Epic, healthcare consulting, confidential employer materials or employment data. No licensed or copied competitor material is used.
